# Moat Scorecard — Section 7 spec

The scorecard answers one question the rest of the report does not: **is the advantage
durable, or is it just this cycle?** It is not a quality-of-execution score and not a
buy/sell signal — it is the durability of the franchise, scored on evidence.

**Placement:** `## 7. Moat Scorecard`, between Strategy & Management Vision (6) and
Valuation (8). The Bottom Line gains a **Moat** lens between Open question and Verdict.

---

## The ten parameters (fixed order, default weights)

| # | Parameter | Weight | What earns a 4–5 | What caps it at 1–2 |
|---|---|---:|---|---|
| 1 | Does something genuinely unique | 15% | Proprietary process or integration peers must buy; patents that survived a challenge; a measured performance edge | Buys the key input; competes on price and lead time |
| 2 | Can't easily copy | 15% | 3+ years and heavy capex to replicate; long qualification/certification cycle; real switching cost | Copyable in a season; surplus industry capacity |
| 3 | Quality over quantity | 10% | Value per unit rising — attach rate, mix, ASP — not just revenue | Growth bought with discounting |
| 4 | Discipline on volume | 5% | Walks away from low-return tenders; margin holds through the downcycle | Capacity-led growth at any price |
| 5 | Customer satisfaction / retention | 10% | Multi-year renewals, repeat orders, second-source qualification, rising wallet share | One-off project revenue; no repeat business |
| 6 | R&D investment | 10% | Patents, labs, commercialised products, litigation wins, disclosed R&D intensity | No disclosed spend and no product proof |
| 7 | Free cash flow | 15% | FCF positive through the cycle; cash conversion above 70% of EBITDA | Negative FCF funded by equity |
| 8 | Debt management | 10% | Net cash, investment-grade rating, equity raised before capex | Refinancing risk; covenants |
| 9 | Management stability | 5% | 5+ year tenure, consistent capital plan, no governance flags | Revolving door; strategy reset every year |
| 10 | Capital allocation | 5% | ROIC above WACC across the cycle; right sequence of equity → debt → capex | Value-destructive M&A or perpetual dilution |

Weights sum to 100. Do not re-weight per company — the weights are the point: **FCF at 15%
and copy resistance at 15%** stop a technology story from scoring well on novelty alone.

## Scoring rubric

| Score | Meaning |
|---:|---|
| **5** | Best-in-industry, with a named proof point |
| **4** | Clearly above peer average, evidenced |
| **3** | Average or adequate; evidence mixed |
| **2** | Weak, or contradicted by the record |
| **1** | Absent, or a negative |

Rules:
- **One line of named evidence per row** — a filing, patent, ruling, order, or disclosed metric.
- **No evidence, no score above 2.** Never score 3+ on customer satisfaction without a
  retention proof point (renewal, repeat order, second-source qualification), or on R&D
  without a patent/product/litigation proof point.
- **Never invent** an NPS, churn rate, R&D-intensity or market-share number the filings do not
  disclose. Write "not disclosed" and score on what *is* disclosed.
- Total = **weighted average, one decimal** — never a simple sum.
- Score **durability**, not recent execution. If the five-year record contradicts the score,
  say so: that gap is the insight, not an embarrassment.

## Bands

| Weighted score | Band |
|---|---|
| 4.0–5.0 | Wide moat — survives a full cycle without management effort |
| 3.0–3.9 | Narrow moat — real advantage, but cyclical and capex-hungry |
| 2.0–2.9 | Eroding advantage — economics depend on the cycle, not the franchise |
| Below 2.0 | No moat — a price-taker |

## Required closing elements

1. `**Total: X.X / 5 — <band>.**`
2. **What the score says** — 3–4 bullets; at least one must name the **binding constraint**
   (usually the lowest weighted score), and one must reconcile score vs record.
3. **What would move the score** — one `Up:` line and one `Down:` line.
4. `> **Moat verdict:**` — one sentence naming the moat's *type* (technical, brand,
   distribution, switching cost, regulatory) and what the price assumes about it.

## Worked render (reference — Sterlite Technologies, Oct 2026)

```
## 7. Moat Scorecard

A moat is what lets a company earn above its cost of capital for longer than one cycle.
STL's five-year record — revenue −0.3% p.a., two loss years — says the advantage has not
protected returns yet. This section scores the durability of the advantage, on ten
parameters, separately from the quality of recent execution.

| Parameter | Weight | Score /5 | Evidence | Read |
|---|---:|---:|---|---|
| **Does something genuinely unique** | 15% | **4.5** | Full preform → fibre → cable chain; hollow-core fibre (−47% latency); G.654.E fibre (30% lower loss); 785+ patents | 🟢 |
| **Can't easily copy** | 15% | **3.5** | Preform lines take years and heavy capex; hyperscaler qualification is a multi-year audit — but cable is copyable and Chinese supply is long | 🟡 |
| **Quality over quantity** | 10% | **3.0** | Attach rate targeted >20% now and 25% by FY27; data centres ~53% of revenue | 🟡 |
| **Discipline on volume** | 5% | **2.0** | The plan is explicitly volume: ₹20,000 Cr by FY29 and +50% capacity | 🔴 |
| **Customer satisfaction / retention** | 10% | **3.5** | Hyperscaler second-source qualification, repeat Neuralis orders, a further US$288 Mn cable agreement; no churn data disclosed | 🟡 |
| **R&D investment** | 10% | **3.5** | 785+ patents, five labs, an EPO appeal that revoked Fujikura's patent outright; R&D not a separate P&L line | 🟡 |
| **Free cash flow** | 15% | **1.5** | Not disclosed; capex ₹1,000 Cr/yr vs ₹756 Cr PAT; net cash came from a ₹1,500 Cr QIP | 🔴 |
| **Debt management** | 10% | **4.0** | Net cash ₹703 Cr; borrowings ₹989 Cr; equity raised before capex; ICRA AA (stable) | 🟢 |
| **Management stability** | 5% | **3.5** | MD at STL since 2007, CEO since 2021; promoter family chairs the board; plan consistent across three calls | 🟡 |
| **Capital allocation** | 5% | **3.0** | Right sequence this cycle; five-year record is −0.3% revenue CAGR and two loss years | 🟡 |

**Total: 3.3 / 5 — narrow moat.**
```

Then the band table, the four "what the score says" bullets (including *the moat is
technical, not commercial* and *the two lowest scores decide the stock*), the up/down lines,
and:

```
> **Moat verdict:** STL owns a real piece of the optical chain — the preform, the fibre
> recipe and the patents around them — and that is worth a narrow moat, not a wide one.
> At 3.3/5 the franchise is better than its five-year record; ₹827 assumes the record has
> already caught up.
```

## Anti-patterns

- **Scoring novelty as a moat.** A clever product with no pricing power is a feature, not a moat.
- **Letting a technology score hide a cash score.** If FCF is 1.5, the section must say the
  moat cannot be converted into cash — do not average it away in the prose.
- **Simple average.** Always weight; a 5 on management (5%) must not offset a 1 on FCF (15%).
- **Flattery.** Most manufacturers score 2.5–3.5. A 4+ requires a named, defensible edge.
- **Copying the verdict from the valuation.** The moat can be narrow while the stock is cheap,
  or wide while it is dear — say both if both are true.
