## TL;DR

- **What it is:** India's largest maker of military training simulators and anti-drone systems. It has 30+ years of IP, 230+ patents and 1,000+ systems shipped.
- **Where it stands:** Revenue fell **29% in FY26** and another **10.5% in Q1 FY27** because of contract timing, not lost demand. The order book (~₹1,417 Cr executable) points to a strong Q2–Q3.
- **Balance sheet:** Debt-free, with about **₹1,198 Cr net cash** (~8% of market cap).
- **The problem is price:** At **84× earnings and 8× book**, the stock already assumes management's ₹4,000 Cr two-year guidance lands. Every AlphaSpread model puts fair value **16–39% below** today's price.
- **Takeaway:** It is a good business priced for perfection. Track new orders and operating margin each quarter.

## 1. What the Company Does

Zen (founded 1993) **designs and manufactures combat training simulators and counter-drone systems.** Its customers are the Army, Navy, Air Force, state police and paramilitary forces. It has offices in Hyderabad, the UAE and the USA.

**How it makes money:** It wins defence tenders, often as the *only* technically qualified bidder under "Buy Indian-IDDM" rules. It then bills in milestones as it delivers. After each sale it earns recurring maintenance (AMC) revenue.

**Why it's different:** Zen is not a build-to-print contractor. It owns the designs, code and drawings. That qualification is the moat, and it supports gross margins of ~73%.

**Three things to understand:**

1. **Single-vendor advantage.** In recent anti-drone and simulator tenders, Zen was the only qualified bidder. After Operation Sindoor, the government placed ~₹800 Cr of anti-drone orders with Zen.
2. **Lumpy revenue.** Milestone billing makes quarters uneven. Q4 FY26 alone was 26% of the year's revenue. Single quarters, especially March and June, can mislead.
3. **Wider portfolio.** Revenue fell in FY26, but the company now has five offerable capabilities: simulators, counter-drone, automated weapon stations, robotics and drones.

**Method:** Revenue growth → PAT margin → EPS → exit P/E → share price. Share count is held at ~9.03 Cr (no dilution assumed).

| **Annual return** | **−15.3%** | **+16.7%** | **+31.3%** |
| FY31 market cap | ₹6,622 Cr | ₹32,886 Cr | ₹59,375 Cr |

**What each scenario means:**
- **Bear:** Tenders stay scarce, margins stay near Q1 levels, and the stock de-rates to 35×.
- **Base:** The book executes and DAC orders convert gradually. Growth stays well below management's guide.
- **Bull:** Large DAC orders land, ₹700–800 Cr of simulator orders arrive, Air Force entry succeeds, and the market keeps a 68× multiple.

### Price Path

| ₹/share | Now | FY27 | FY28 | FY29 | FY30 | FY31 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Bear | 1,682 | 1,425 | 1,207 | 1,022 | 866 | 733 |

| Bull | 1,682 | 2,209 | 2,902 | 3,811 | 5,006 | 6,575 |

### Two Important Insights

1. **Even the Bull case falls short of management's guidance.** The ₹4,000 Cr FY27+28 target means revenue roughly doubling each year (~₹1,350 Cr in FY27, ~₹2,650 Cr in FY28). The Bull case delivers only ~₹2,200 Cr over those two years. If management hits its guide, the stock is likely cheap. If it doesn't, the current price leans heavily on the guidance being credible.
2. **Earnings matter more than the multiple.** Bear earnings at a Base multiple give ~₹1,155. Base earnings at a Bear multiple give ~₹2,317. Order conversion is the first thing to watch; re-rating comes second.
